# LORE Volume 28 - Governance, Stewardship, and Ecosystem Evolution

## Version 0.2 Draft

---

# 1. Purpose

This volume explores how LORE may evolve as a shared ecosystem.

The purpose is not to define a centralized authority structure.

The purpose is to identify:

- stewardship responsibilities,
- governance boundaries,
- ecosystem coordination,
- and long-term sustainability concerns.

---

# 2. Core Principle

The governing principle:

> A trust ecosystem requires governance, but governance must not become the source of all trust.

---

# 3. Governance Problem

Any foundational system faces a tension:

Too little governance:

- fragmentation,
- incompatible interpretations,
- security failures.

Too much governance:

- centralization,
- reduced innovation,
- slow evolution.

---

# 4. Governance Layers

LORE may require multiple governance layers.

Potential layers:

```text
Core Semantics

|

Reference Implementations

|

Domain Extensions

|

Local Deployments
```

---

# 5. Core Stewardship

Responsibilities may include:

- maintaining core definitions,
- preserving semantic consistency,
- documenting changes,
- managing compatibility.

---

# 6. What Core Governance Should Not Do

Core governance should not attempt to:

- define every domain,
- control every implementation,
- become a universal authority,
- decide every trust relationship.

---

# 7. Domain Governance

Domains may govern:

- domain-specific relationships,
- domain assertions,
- specialized evidence,
- operational practices.

Examples:

- healthcare,
- OT,
- enterprise security,
- personal environments.

---

# 8. Local Authority Principle

A LORE universe should retain local authority.

A local root determines:

- local identifiers,
- local relationships,
- local assertions,
- local capabilities.

---

# 9. Federation Governance

Federation requires agreement about:

- discovery,
- verification,
- trust establishment,
- revocation,
- interoperability.

---

# 10. Federation Does Not Mean Merger

Important distinction:

```text
Federation

≠

Centralization
```

Independent universes may cooperate while preserving autonomy.

---

# 11. Extension Governance

Extensions should identify:

- owner,
- purpose,
- compatibility requirements,
- lifecycle.

---

# 12. Relationship Family Governance

Relationship families are likely to become an important governance surface.

Questions:

- Who defines common families?
- How are inverses defined?
- How are deprecated relationships handled?

---

# 13. Semantic Drift

A major risk:

Two systems use the same term but mean different things.

Example:

```text
"Trusted"

System A:

authenticated source

System B:

authorized actor
```

---

# 14. Naming and Namespace Governance

Names create social and technical risks.

Potential concerns:

- collisions,
- ambiguity,
- ownership disputes,
- historical changes.

---

# 15. Version Governance

LORE should support:

- semantic versioning,
- migration paths,
- compatibility documentation.

---

# 16. Deprecation Governance

A mature ecosystem must retire concepts safely.

A deprecated concept should include:

- reason,
- replacement,
- migration guidance,
- historical interpretation.

---

# 17. Security Governance

Security-related governance includes:

- vulnerability handling,
- cryptographic changes,
- emergency response,
- security reviews.

---

# 18. Root Recovery Governance

Because roots are important trust anchors, recovery procedures require special attention.

Potential mechanisms:

- offline recovery,
- multi-party authorization,
- emergency capabilities,
- documented procedures.

---

# 19. Open Source Governance

Potential benefits:

- transparency,
- review,
- experimentation,
- ecosystem participation.

Potential challenges:

- fragmentation,
- incompatible forks,
- unclear authority.

---

# 20. Standards Relationship

LORE should consider standards carefully.

Standards provide:

- interoperability,
- shared vocabulary,
- confidence.

Standards can also create:

- rigidity,
- barriers,
- premature commitments.

---

# 21. Adoption Without Control

A successful ecosystem may require:

- reference implementations,
- documentation,
- test suites,
- examples.

It should not require:

- permission from a central organization.

---

# 22. Community Feedback Loop

A healthy ecosystem requires:

- criticism,
- experimentation,
- operational feedback.

---

# 23. "Eat What We Make" Principle

LORE governance must apply internally.

If governance creates:

- unnecessary bureaucracy,
- unclear authority,
- excessive complexity,

the design has failed.

---

# 24. Governance Failure Modes

Potential failures:

## Centralization

One authority controls all trust.

---

## Fragmentation

No shared meaning remains.

---

## Capture

A group controls evolution for narrow interests.

---

## Stagnation

The system cannot evolve.

---

# 25. Review Questions

Reviewers should challenge:

1. Who should govern the core?
2. What authority should governance possess?
3. How are local and global concerns balanced?
4. How are disputes resolved?
5. How does the ecosystem avoid centralization?
6. How does the ecosystem avoid fragmentation?

---

# 26. Governance Principle

The governing principle:

> The purpose of governance is to preserve useful cooperation, not to replace local judgment.
