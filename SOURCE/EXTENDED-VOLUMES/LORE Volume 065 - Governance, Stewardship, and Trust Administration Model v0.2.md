# LORE Volume 65 - Governance, Stewardship, and Trust Administration Model

## Version 0.2 Draft

---

# 1. Purpose

This volume examines how LORE relationships, authorities, and trust information should be governed.

The purpose is not to create centralized control.

The purpose is to define:

- ownership,
- responsibility,
- review,
- change management,
- and accountability.

---

# 2. Core Principle

The governing principle:

> Trust information without stewardship becomes stale information, and stale information becomes dangerous information.

---

# 3. Governance Philosophy

A trust model requires more than technology.

It requires:

- responsible owners,
- clear processes,
- defined authority,
- continuous review.

---

# 4. Stewardship Model

Stewardship means responsibility for maintaining:

- object information,
- relationships,
- assertions,
- evidence,
- authority.

---

# 5. Ownership

Every important object or relationship should have:

- an owner,
- a responsible party,
- a review process.

---

# 6. Ownership vs Control

Important distinction:

```text id="m7q4vx"
Ownership

≠

Administrative Control
```

A person may maintain information without possessing operational authority.

---

# 7. Relationship Stewardship

Relationships require ownership.

Examples:

- device ownership,
- application dependency,
- service trust,
- delegated authority.

---

# 8. Authority Administration

Authority management should include:

- creation,
- approval,
- delegation,
- review,
- expiration,
- revocation.

---

# 9. Separation of Duties

Important principle:

The person who creates a relationship should not always be the person who approves or reviews it.

---

# 10. Governance Roles

Potential roles:

## Object Owner

Responsible for object accuracy.

---

## Relationship Owner

Responsible for relationship validity.

---

## Authority Administrator

Responsible for capability and delegation management.

---

## Reviewer

Responsible for validation.

---

## Auditor

Responsible for independent assessment.

---

# 11. Change Management

Trust relationships change over time.

Changes should include:

- who changed it,
- why it changed,
- when it changed,
- what impact exists.

---

# 12. Review Cycles

Potential review patterns:

- periodic review,
- event-driven review,
- risk-based review.

---

# 13. Risk-Based Governance

Not all relationships require equal attention.

Higher-risk relationships may include:

- broad authority,
- critical systems,
- external dependencies,
- autonomous agents.

---

# 14. Trust Decay

Relationships may become less reliable over time.

Causes:

- organizational changes,
- personnel changes,
- outdated assumptions,
- technology changes.

---

# 15. Relationship Expiration

Important relationships should support:

- expiration dates,
- renewal,
- review requirements.

---

# 16. Emergency Governance

Emergency actions require:

- bounded authority,
- documentation,
- later review.

---

# 17. Federation Governance

Federated relationships require:

- trust agreements,
- ownership,
- revocation mechanisms,
- conflict resolution.

---

# 18. Governance Automation

Automation may assist with:

- reminders,
- stale relationship detection,
- impact analysis,
- approval workflows.

Automation should not remove accountability.

---

# 19. Governance Failure Modes

Potential failures:

## Abandoned Relationships

No one maintains them.

---

## Ownership Ambiguity

No responsible party exists.

---

## Authority Accumulation

Privileges expand over time.

---

## Review Fatigue

Important relationships are not examined.

---

# 20. Governance Metrics

Potential measurements:

- stale relationship count,
- expired authority count,
- review completion,
- unresolved ownership.

---

# 21. Governance Questions

Reviewers should challenge:

1. Who owns trust information?
2. Who validates it?
3. How does it stay current?
4. How are disputes resolved?
5. How are mistakes corrected?

---

# 22. Governance Principle

The governing principle:

> Trust is not a static property. It is a relationship that requires ongoing care.
