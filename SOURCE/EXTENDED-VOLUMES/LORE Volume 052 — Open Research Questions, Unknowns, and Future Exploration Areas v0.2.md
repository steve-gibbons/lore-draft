# LORE Volume 52 — Open Research Questions, Unknowns, and Future Exploration Areas

## Version 0.2 Draft

---

# 1. Purpose

This volume identifies areas where LORE requires additional investigation.

The purpose is not to present unanswered questions as failures.

The purpose is to make uncertainty explicit.

---

# 2. Core Principle

The governing principle:

> Unknowns that are visible can be investigated. Unknowns that are hidden become assumptions.

---

# 3. Research Philosophy

LORE should maintain a distinction between:

- established concepts,
- design hypotheses,
- unresolved questions,
- experimental ideas.

---

# 4. Fundamental Question

The primary research question:

> Is semantic trust context a missing abstraction in modern computing?

---

# 5. Abstraction Boundary Questions

Open questions:

- What belongs inside LORE?
- What belongs outside LORE?
- What concepts are foundational?
- What concepts are domain-specific?

---

# 6. Ontology Questions

Research areas:

- Are the proposed primitives sufficient?
- Are any primitives redundant?
- Are relationships first-class objects?
- Are assertions and evidence sufficiently separated?

---

# 7. Trust Representation Questions

Open questions:

- Can trust relationships be represented consistently?
- How much context is required?
- How much context is too much?
- Can trust remain understandable at scale?

---

# 8. Authority Questions

Research areas:

- How should authority be represented?
- How should authority delegation work?
- How should authority expiration work?
- How should emergency authority be handled?

---

# 9. Capability Questions

Open questions:

- Should capabilities be transferable?
- How should capabilities be constrained?
- How should capability use be recorded?
- How should misuse be detected?

---

# 10. Evidence Questions

Research areas:

- What qualifies as evidence?
- How should evidence quality be represented?
- How should conflicting evidence be handled?
- How should evidence decay over time?

---

# 11. Provenance Questions

Open questions:

- How much provenance is useful?
- When does provenance become excessive?
- Can provenance itself become a security risk?
- How should provenance be verified?

---

# 12. Lifecycle Questions

Research areas:

- Which objects should be permanent?
- Which relationships should expire?
- How should history be preserved?
- How should recovery work?

---

# 13. Federation Questions

Open questions:

- How do independent trust universes interact?
- How is foreign trust evaluated?
- How are conflicts resolved?
- How are federation relationships revoked?

---

# 14. Scale Questions

Research areas:

- Does the model remain useful at internet scale?
- Does graph complexity become unmanageable?
- What information should remain local?
- What information can be globally discoverable?

---

# 15. Performance Questions

Open questions:

- What must be resolved in real time?
- What can be cached?
- What requires authoritative lookup?
- What verification costs are acceptable?

---

# 16. Privacy Questions

Research areas:

- How can relationships be visible without excessive disclosure?
- Can trust decisions be made with limited disclosure?
- How can metadata leakage be minimized?

---

# 17. Agent Questions

Open questions:

- Are agents sufficiently different from existing principals?
- What is the correct authority model for autonomous systems?
- How should intent be represented?
- How should agent decisions be explained?

---

# 18. OT Questions

Research areas:

- How should physical consequences affect trust?
- How should safety boundaries interact with authority?
- How should local control override remote relationships?

---

# 19. Cryptographic Questions

Potential research:

- signatures,
- verifiable credentials,
- zero-knowledge proofs,
- secure enclaves,
- cryptographic agility.

---

# 20. Implementation Questions

Open questions:

- What is the smallest useful implementation?
- What components are unnecessary?
- Which implementation patterns naturally emerge?

---

# 21. Standards Questions

Research areas:

- Should LORE define standards?
- Should LORE use existing standards?
- When is standardization appropriate?

---

# 22. Economic Questions

Potential considerations:

- Who operates infrastructure?
- Who maintains trust relationships?
- Who pays for verification?
- What incentives encourage adoption?

---

# 23. Social Questions

Technology alone cannot solve:

- poor governance,
- bad incentives,
- unclear accountability.

Questions:

- How do organizations use trust information responsibly?
- How do humans interpret automated decisions?

---

# 24. Failure Research

Important question:

> What failure modes have not yet been imagined?

Potential methods:

- adversarial review,
- simulations,
- incident analysis,
- prototype deployments.

---

# 25. Prototype Research

Useful experiments may include:

- personal trust universe,
- enterprise asset model,
- agent security envelope,
- OT demonstration environment,
- federated resolver prototype.

---

# 26. Metrics Research

Potential measurements:

- reduction in ambiguity,
- improved decision quality,
- reduced privilege exposure,
- faster incident response,
- improved explanation quality.

---

# 27. Research Anti-Goals

Avoid optimizing:

- theoretical completeness,
- maximum abstraction,
- complexity,
- novelty alone.

---

# 28. Research Questions for Reviewers

1. What question should LORE answer that current systems cannot?
2. What research already addresses this?
3. What assumptions require evidence?
4. What experiment would provide the most information?
5. What result would invalidate the model?

---

# 29. Closing Principle

The governing principle:

> A useful architecture is not defined by the answers it already has. It is defined by the quality of the questions it makes possible.
