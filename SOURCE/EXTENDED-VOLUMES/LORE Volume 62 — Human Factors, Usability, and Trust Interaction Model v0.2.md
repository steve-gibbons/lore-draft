# LORE Volume 62 — Human Factors, Usability, and Trust Interaction Model

## Version 0.2 Draft

---

# 1. Purpose

This volume examines the relationship between LORE and human decision-making.

The purpose is to recognize that trust systems ultimately exist to support people and organizations.

A technically correct system that humans cannot understand or operate will fail.

---

# 2. Core Principle

The governing principle:

> A trust model must be understandable by the people responsible for making decisions based on it.

---

# 3. Human Factors Philosophy

LORE should account for:

- human judgment,
- human limitations,
- organizational behavior,
- operational realities.

---

# 4. Humans as Trust Participants

Humans may act as:

- principals,
- authorities,
- reviewers,
- administrators,
- consumers of explanations.

---

# 5. The Human Decision Boundary

A key question:

> Which decisions should be automated, and which should remain human responsibilities?

---

# 6. Automation Principle

Automation should improve:

- consistency,
- speed,
- visibility,
- repeatability.

Automation should not remove:

- accountability,
- oversight,
- appropriate judgment.

---

# 7. Explainability Requirements

A LORE-enabled decision should provide understandable answers:

- What decision was made?
- Why was it made?
- What information influenced it?
- Who was responsible?
- What alternatives existed?

---

# 8. Explanation Quality

A useful explanation should be:

## Accurate

The explanation reflects the actual decision process.

---

## Relevant

The explanation includes meaningful factors.

---

## Understandable

The explanation can be interpreted by the intended audience.

---

## Actionable

The explanation helps someone decide what to do next.

---

# 9. Avoiding Explanation Overload

More information does not always create better understanding.

Potential failure:

```text id="m7q4vx"
More Data

≠

More Understanding
```

---

# 10. Cognitive Load

LORE should consider:

- amount of information presented,
- complexity of relationships,
- urgency of decisions,
- expertise of the user.

---

# 11. Audience Models

Different users require different views.

---

## Operators

Need:

- current state,
- impact,
- recommended action.

---

## Security Analysts

Need:

- relationships,
- history,
- evidence,
- authority paths.

---

## Executives

Need:

- risk,
- business impact,
- strategic context.

---

## Developers

Need:

- APIs,
- relationships,
- implementation guidance.

---

# 12. Trust Visualization

Potential approaches:

- relationship graphs,
- authority paths,
- timelines,
- evidence chains.

---

# 13. Visualization Risks

Visualizations can mislead.

Potential problems:

- excessive complexity,
- false confidence,
- hidden assumptions.

---

# 14. Human Trust Calibration

LORE should avoid encouraging:

- blind trust in automation,
- blind trust in evidence,
- blind trust in reputation.

---

# 15. The Automation Paradox

A system intended to reduce ambiguity can create new ambiguity if users do not understand:

- what it knows,
- what it assumes,
- what it does not know.

---

# 16. Human Override

Human override may be necessary.

However:

Override should include:

- justification,
- authority,
- expiration,
- accountability.

---

# 17. Emergency Decisions

Emergency conditions require:

- speed,
- bounded authority,
- later review.

---

# 18. Organizational Culture

LORE adoption depends on:

- trust in the process,
- willingness to document relationships,
- willingness to challenge assumptions.

---

# 19. Human Failure Modes

Potential failures:

## Assumption Acceptance

Users assume the system is always correct.

---

## Relationship Neglect

Users fail to maintain context.

---

## Alert Fatigue

Users ignore important information.

---

## Process Avoidance

Users bypass controls.

---

# 20. Training Requirements

Users need understanding of:

- trust concepts,
- authority concepts,
- evidence concepts,
- limitations.

---

# 21. Design Principle

LORE should make the right action easier.

It should not merely make the correct action possible.

---

# 22. Human-Centered Validation

Evaluation should measure:

- comprehension,
- decision accuracy,
- time to resolution,
- user confidence.

---

# 23. Human Factors Questions

Reviewers should challenge:

1. Can people understand LORE decisions?
2. Will users trust it appropriately?
3. Will users misuse it?
4. Does it reduce cognitive burden?
5. Does it create new complexity?

---

# 24. Closing Principle

The governing principle:

> Trust systems are ultimately human systems. Their success depends not only on technical correctness, but on whether people can understand, question, and responsibly use them.
